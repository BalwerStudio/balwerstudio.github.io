OVERVIEW

Designing a mobile experience around speed, clarity, and confidence.

Daleely was approached as a mobile-first product where the quality of the experience depends heavily on navigation clarity and task flow. The goal was to reduce unnecessary decisions and make the next action easy to understand at each stage.

Rather than treating the project as a collection of isolated screens, the design focused on how users move between discovery, details, action, saved content, and return visits.

01Mobile-first

Layouts and interactions were planned around handheld use from the start.

02Focused actions

Each screen uses one clear primary action to reduce hesitation.

03Consistent patterns

Cards, navigation, and controls follow a reusable visual system.

04Simple hierarchy

Important information appears first, supporting content second.

THE CHALLENGE

Mobile interfaces become difficult when every element competes for attention.

01

Too many choices at once

Users need a clear priority between browsing, selecting, and taking action.

02

Weak navigation hierarchy

Important destinations should remain predictable across the whole product.

03

Information overload

Detail screens need enough context without becoming long, dense, or visually tiring.

OUTCOME & LEARNINGS

Good mobile UX is often about removing decisions, not adding features.

Daleely strengthened my focus on mobile hierarchy and flow consistency. On smaller screens, every visual decision has more impact because there is less space for competing information.

The project reinforced a simple principle: each screen should help users understand what matters now, what they can do next, and how to return if they are not ready to act yet.
